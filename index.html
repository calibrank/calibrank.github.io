When Calibration Data Become Training Data

Specialization and Self-Generated Text in Learned Low-Rank Compression of Large Language Models

Van Tien Pham Faculty of Information Technology, Ton Duc Thang University, Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam
Read the paper PDF Code and run records Cite

The paper in one minute

Calibration data used to be a statistic. Now they are training data.

Large language models can be shrunk by replacing each weight matrix with the product of two thin ones. Which directions to drop is decided by a small calibration set: a few hundred windows of text passed through the model. When the compressed model is then trained to imitate the original on those windows, as in learned subspace projections (LSP), the calibration set stops being a sample from which to estimate statistics and becomes the data the model is fitted on. It is still chosen by convention.

This study compresses OPT-1.3B, Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen3-1.7B by 30% and 50% with LSP, calibrated on six sources at a matched budget, over three seeds, and repeats the comparison on Llama-3.1-8B, with a second learned compressor and on an instruction-tuned model. It does not propose a new compression method; it measures what the choice of calibration data does.

Abstract

Low-rank compression shrinks large language models by factorizing their weights, guided by a small calibration set. When the factorization is learned by distillation, as in learned subspace projections (LSP), the calibration set becomes training data, yet is still chosen by convention. We compressed OPT-1.3B, Llama-3.2-1B, and Qwen3-1.7B by 30% and 50% with LSP, calibrated on six sources at a matched budget (WikiText-2, C4, SlimPajama, Alpaca, and two kinds of self-generated text) over three seeds, and repeated the comparison on Llama-3.1-8B and with a second compressor. Training removed most of the up to 600-fold perplexity differences between untrained models but turned them into specialization: each source best preserved its own text, and WikiText-2, the customary choice, gave the least general models. Zero-shot accuracy slightly favored instruction data, through content rather than template. Self-generated documents gave the most uniformly faithful Llama models without external data, whereas exact samples of the model, whose expected training objective is the sequence-level divergence, failed on Qwen3-1.7B. The initialization’s domain survived training, and compressed Llama models collapsed without their calibration beginning-of-sequence token. With every source, compression lost most instruction following and multi-step reasoning. These results indicate that calibration sources should be reported with every perplexity.

Background

Why a compressor specializes

Pretrained weight matrices are not low-rank, but the activations they act on are. A low-rank compressor therefore needs to know which directions of each layer's input the model actually uses, and it learns this from the calibration windows. A training-free method such as SVD-LLM sees those windows only through their second-order statistics, and reports that their size, seed and source change its results by at most 3%.

A learned method fits them. In LSP the compressed model is optimized to agree with the dense model on the calibration windows and nowhere else, so which data to fit becomes a modeling decision. Feature selection has long known the consequence: a selection fitted to one distribution preserves what that distribution exercises. The toy below shows the mechanism in three dimensions.

Real models are not three-dimensional, and LSP does more than keep the directions of largest variance: it trains the projectors against the dense model's output distribution. The question is whether the same specialization survives that training, and at what cost. In practice the choice is made by convention: WikiText-2 for perplexity tables, and Alpaca or C4 for zero-shot accuracy. The pretraining corpus of an open-weight model is rarely released, and domain data may be proprietary or licensed for research only.

Method

Learned subspace projections

Consider a linear layer \(\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{W}\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}\). To remove \(k\) directions from its input, LSP applies the orthogonal projector \(\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^\top\), where \(\mathbf{U} = \operatorname{qf}(\mathbf{V})\) is the orthonormal factor of a trainable matrix \(\mathbf{V}\) with \(k\) columns. The projected layer has rank at most \(d_\text{in} - k\) and merges exactly into two thin factors:

\[ \mathbf{W}\mathbf{P} = (\mathbf{W}\mathbf{U}_\perp)\,\mathbf{U}_\perp^\top = \mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}, \qquad \mathbf{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{d_\text{out}\times r},\; \mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times d_\text{in}},\; r = d_\text{in}-k. \]

Layers that read the same activation (the query, key and value projections; the gate and up projections) share one projector. After training the projectors merge into the factors, and the deployed model contains no operation specific to LSP: it is an ordinary dense model with smaller matrices.

The projectors start from the whitened truncation of SVD-LLM, computed from the Gram matrix of each layer's input on the calibration windows. They are then trained jointly, with every pretrained weight frozen, to minimize the token-averaged divergence of the compressed model \(q\) from the dense model \(p\) on calibration windows \(\mathbf{x} = (x_1, \dots, x_T)\) drawn from the calibration distribution \(\mathcal{D}\):

\[ \mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{D}}\; \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathrm{KL}\big(p(\cdot\mid x_{\le t}) \,\big\|\, q(\cdot\mid x_{\le t})\big). \]

The calibration set therefore enters at three points:

A training-free method uses only the first. Learned methods use all three, which is why the data matter more to them. Rank allocation follows the uniform rule of LSP and does not depend on the data, except in one control.

Implementation. No implementation of LSP was released; the paper reimplements it in PyTorch. The reimplementation reproduces the published trained results within 6% at the ratios studied, but not the untrained initialization at high ratios. The difference is traced to which side of the square attention-output projections is projected (Appendix B of the paper); it does not change the ranking of calibration sources.

Experimental design

Six calibration sources, one budget

Every source supplies 512 training windows of 2,048 tokens, about one million tokens, plus 32 validation windows. Windows are built identically for every corpus: documents in random order, separated by the end-of-sequence (EOS) token and cut into windows that start with the beginning-of-sequence (BOS) token, for the models that have one.

  • WikiText-2: Wikipedia articles; the usual calibration set of perplexity-oriented compression.
  • C4: filtered web text; the default calibration set of pruning and quantization.
  • SlimPajama: a deduplicated mixture of web crawl, C4, code, books, arXiv, Wikipedia and StackExchange; it approximates a pretraining mixture.
  • Alpaca: instruction–response pairs in the Alpaca template; the calibration set of the zero-shot experiments of SVD-LLM and LSP.
  • Self (seq.): 2,048-token sequences sampled from the dense model itself, exactly, at temperature one.
  • Self (doc.): independent documents sampled from the dense model, each ending at the model's first end-of-text token or after 512 tokens, packed like a corpus.

The sources differ in content and in how their text fills a window. The figure shows the first training windows of the seed-0 runs.

Each compressed model is evaluated by perplexity on WikiText-2, C4 and the seven SlimPajama domains; by its divergence from the dense model on held-out text of every source and of six domains chosen independently of the sources; by zero-shot accuracy on seven tasks; and, for one seed, by open-ended generation, long contexts, passkey retrieval, generative tasks and instruction following. The three-seed repetition records every evaluation item, so differences come with paired-bootstrap intervals.

Theory

Text written by the model itself

The dense model can supply its own calibration data. For the distillation objective of LSP this is more than a substitute for real text. Write \(P_T\) and \(Q_T\) for the distributions that the dense and the compressed model assign to whole sequences. By the chain rule of the Kullback–Leibler divergence,

\[ \mathrm{KL}(P_T \,\|\, Q_T) \;=\; \sum_{t=1}^{T} \mathbb{E}_{x_{\le t}\sim P}\; \mathrm{KL}\big(p(\cdot\mid x_{\le t}) \,\big\|\, q(\cdot\mid x_{\le t})\big). \]
Proposition 1

If the calibration windows are drawn from the dense model by ancestral sampling at temperature one, the expected training objective equals the sequence-level divergence per token, \(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim P}\,\mathcal{L} = \mathrm{KL}(P_T\,\|\,Q_T)/T\). For windows drawn from any other distribution, the objective weighs the same per-context divergences by that distribution's contexts instead.

The sequence-level divergence is the natural target when the compressed model will replace the dense one for generation. The paper uses the identity to show which divergence each source optimizes, not as a theoretical contribution. You can check it on a model small enough to enumerate:

Two caveats come with the principle. The proposition is about the population objective: in practice the windows form a pool reused over several epochs, and the projectors are fitted to the pool. And long samples of small models drift. The entropy of Llama-3.2-1B's predictions on its own samples grows from 2.6 nats over the first 128 positions to 5.7 over the last 128; Qwen3-1.7B drifts from 3.2 to 4.7 as its text degenerates into high-entropy strings. OPT-1.3B does not drift. Self-generated documents, restarted from a fresh start token at least every 512 tokens, avoid the drift.

Temperature one is also the right temperature in practice: sampling the exact sequences of Llama-3.2-1B at 0.7 or 1.3 instead more than doubles the WikiText-2 and C4 perplexities of the model compressed by 50% (WikiText-2: 123 and 120 against 53.2).

Results

What the calibration data change

The main grid compresses three models at two ratios with six sources, with one run each; it is repeated with three seeds and an extended evaluation. All figures below are drawn from the run records; switch models and ratios to see each result in every setting the paper reports.

4.1–4.2Training turns a lottery into specialization

Before training, the whitened initialization depends heavily on the calibration source: at −30% its perplexity varies across sources by a factor of 2.7 to 7.3 on OPT-1.3B, 18 to 108 on Llama-3.2-1B and 4 to 32 on Qwen3-1.7B. The oblique truncation of SVD-LLM depends less on the source, but in the same way, and far more than the 3% SVD-LLM reports for an older model at a lower ratio.

TakeawayA WikiText-2 perplexity measured after WikiText-2 calibration, the most common number in the low-rank compression literature, measures fit to the calibration domain as much as compression quality.

4.3Each source preserves its own text

The divergence from the dense model on held-out text of every source makes the specialization plain. On the held-out set of each real corpus, the divergence is lowest when calibrating on that corpus, for every model and ratio, with one near-tie. Over the three seeds, with 128 held-out windows per set, the matching source has the lowest divergence in 86 of 90 comparisons; the four exceptions are ties between SlimPajama and self-generated documents on SlimPajama text, on Llama-3.2-1B. Away from the diagonal, narrow sources are costly.

Which source is the most uniformly faithful depends on what it is measured on, which is why the panel lets you weigh the sets. Over the five held-out sets, self-generated documents have the lowest geometric-mean relative divergence in all six settings (tied with C4 on OPT-1.3B at −50%), and they stay the most common winner when the sets are weighted at random. Without the held-out samples of the dense model, which favor self-generated calibration, they remain the most uniform only on Llama-3.2-1B; C4 is more uniform on OPT-1.3B and SlimPajama on Qwen3-1.7B. On the six independent domains they are the best source for every domain on Llama-3.2-1B and within 16% of the best on Qwen3-1.7B, while SlimPajama is clearly the most uniform on OPT-1.3B. On Llama-3.1-8B they are the most uniform by all three summaries.

A matched-budget mixture of SlimPajama and self-generated documents, 256 windows each, combines the two. It is the most uniform of the seven calibrations on the four real held-out sets in five of the six settings and tied in the sixth, and it is never more than 11% less uniform than the best calibration, in any setting or summary.

4.3Where specialization costs most

Code and multilingual Wikipedia suffer most from narrow English calibration. At −30%, WikiText-2 calibration multiplies the code perplexity of Llama-3.2-1B by 144 relative to the dense model, and Alpaca multiplies its multilingual Wikipedia perplexity by 97, while SlimPajama and self-generated documents keep every domain within a factor of 7. Calibrating on a single domain takes the specialization further, and pays for it everywhere else.

4.2, 4.9Zero-shot accuracy favors instruction data, a little

Accuracy varies much less across sources than perplexity: by 1.7 to 3.5 points on OPT-1.3B and Llama-3.2-1B and 5.0 to 5.4 on Qwen3-1.7B, leaving aside Qwen3's failed exact self-samples at −30%. Without BoolQ, Alpaca is the most accurate source on every model and ratio, though its perplexities are among the worst, and WikiText-2 gives the lowest accuracy of the real sources. BoolQ needs care: most compressed models score below its majority-class rate of 62.2%, the accuracy of always answering yes, so differences on it reflect shifts in answer bias more than reading comprehension.

4.4Where the data act: initialization and windows

The calibration data act at two stages, the Gram statistics of the initialization and the training windows, and crossing them separates the two. Both matter, and the domain of the initialization survives training. The removed subspaces show why: training moves them very little. The overlap between the subspace a unit removes before and after training averages 0.996 to 0.997 for every model and ratio, while subspaces initialized from different sources overlap only 0.85 to 0.90 and do not come closer during training, even over a schedule three times as long.

4.5More distinct windows keep helping

At a fixed budget of 512 steps, more distinct calibration windows improve both C4 and self-generated documents, up to 4,096 windows each seen once. Calibration budgets that suffice for training-free methods do not saturate learned ones. The learning curves show what repetition does: with 64 windows the projectors fit the windows they see far more closely than other text of the same source, a gap that closes at 4,096.

4.6When the model's own samples fail

Exact self-samples are the one source that fails, and on one model. On Qwen3-1.7B the compressed model calibrated on its own long samples is a poor model of real text in every seed, with a severity that varies from run to run, although every run matches the dense model on held-out samples about as well as self-generated documents do. The objective of Proposition 1 is met, but it leaves the behavior on real text loosely constrained.

5.5The first token

For the Llama models the format of the calibration windows matters as much as their content. Every compressed Llama model collapses on inputs that do not start with the BOS token, whatever its calibration source; the dense models are insensitive to the difference. A compressed model served without its BOS token, as in batched or streaming inference that splits a document, would be unusable, although every standard benchmark, which adds BOS, would rate it normally.

4.4–4.10What survives the controls

A comparison of sources could be confounded by how the data are presented and optimized rather than by what they say. The paper tests each alternative explanation in turn. The specialization survives all of them; each line below is recomputed from the records.

4.10A larger model behaves the same way

Llama-3.1-8B compressed with all six sources, three seeds at −30% and one at −50%, shows the same pattern with wider gaps. Before training the sources differ up to 594-fold at −50%; after training WikiText-2 calibration gives the lowest WikiText-2 perplexity and C4 the lowest C4 perplexity, narrow sources pay elsewhere (WikiText-2 calibration leaves code at 220 times the dense perplexity at −30%), and self-generated documents are again the most uniformly faithful source.

4.11, 5.4Fidelity is not utility

The comparisons rank calibration sources for a given compressor, ratio and budget; they do not show that any compressed model can replace its dense model. Beyond perplexity and multiple choice, compression costs more, and the calibration source matters as much. Abilities that the calibration windows do not exercise can disappear: after Alpaca calibration, the compressed Llama-3.2-1B no longer retrieves a passkey from its context. Given more tokens, it continues the prompt with generic text, “the key to the door”, instead of copying the key.

Discussion

Choosing calibration data

Two consequences follow for reporting. A perplexity measured on the corpus the model was calibrated on overstates its quality elsewhere, so the calibration source should accompany every reported perplexity, and comparisons between methods should hold it fixed. And no single number summarizes a compressed model: perplexity rewards the matching domain, zero-shot accuracy rewards instruction data, and the two disagree on which source is best. Measuring the divergence from the dense model on several held-out sources exposes the trade-off directly and needs no labels.

Limitations. The main experiments compress models of 1.2 to 1.7 billion parameters, with a check on an 8-billion-parameter model; larger models were out of reach of one GPU. All corpora are English and the zero-shot benchmarks are multiple-choice; behavior is evaluated with one seed and one ratio, and instruction following on one 1.2-billion-parameter model. One learned method is studied in depth and checked against a second on two models at one ratio. The controls are single runs. The role of the first token is supported by the density of BOS tokens and by an intervention on the calibration format, not by an analysis of attention circuits.
